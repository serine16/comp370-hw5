# Task 3: NYC 311 Complaint Type Analysis (January–February vs. June–July 2024)

## Overview
This analysis examines the seasonal distribution of the most frequently reported 311 complaint type in New York City for 2024, comparing winter operations (January–February) against summer operations (June–July).

## The Top Complaint
The overall most abundant complaint type during the first two months of 2024 (January and February) was **HEAT/HOT WATER**. 

## The Numbers: Seasonal Comparison
* January – February 2024:** A total of 80,415 `HEAT/HOT WATER` complaints were logged.
* June – July 2024: Total complaints for the same category dropped drastically to **6,766**.

## Explanation of Findings
The massive discrepancy between winter and summer complaint volumes is a direct result of seasonal weather changes and NYC housing regulations. New York City legally mandates that landlords provide adequate heat and hot water during the designated heating season. During January and February, freezing outdoor temperatures cause frequent boiler and heating system failures, resulting in massive spikes in tenant complaints. In contrast, during the warm summer months of June and July, heating systems are inactive, causing complaint numbers to plummet to a small fraction of winter volumes.

## Reference to the Plot & Spikes
The daily bar chart generated for January and February 2024 shows intense daily fluctuations, with peak counts exceeding 3,500 complaints in a single day. These major spikes align precisely with severe winter cold snaps—most notably around mid-January (January 16–18), when extreme sub-freezing temperatures hit the city and triggered widespread heating outages.
